1 · What the prototype does

It turns raw satellite imagery of the North Indian Ocean into early, explained and actionable cyclone guidance: it finds cyclonic systems in the whole-basin image, classifies their intensity on IMD's scale, warns of genesis and rapid intensification, predicts track and intensity with a calibrated uncertainty cone, and converts the forecast into landfall, exposure and district-level alerts in the Common Alerting Protocol (CAP) format.

The problem it addresses

  • Open ocean has almost no ground observations — satellites are the main source of truth for storms over the Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea.
  • Rapid intensification (RI) — winds rising ≥30 kt in 24 h — is the hardest forecast problem and leaves the least time for evacuation (Ockhi 2017, Biparjoy 2023). RI is rare (≈1 in 15 observations), so naive models ignore it.
  • Data scarcity: the North Indian Ocean has far fewer historical storms than the Pacific, which limits machine learning.
  • Uncertainty is rarely communicated: a single forecast line looks precise but misleads decision-makers.
  • The last mile: a forecast only saves lives when it reaches the right district and fishing community in their language.

2 · How it solves the problem

Figure 2 — From one satellite image to one actionable alert
  1. Satellite scene. Every 3 hours the whole-basin infrared (IR) and water-vapour (WV) image is read. Cold cloud tops = deep convection.
  2. Identify. The basin detector marks every cyclone or depression centre and links them over time into tracks. A track is "confirmed" after persisting 15 h, which filters most false alarms.
  3. Classify. A 14°×14° crop around each centre goes to the intensity CNN, which returns three cross-checked estimates — wind speed, Dvorak T-number (converted with the standard Dvorak table) and IMD category — plus a Grad-CAM heat map showing which clouds drove the estimate.
  4. Warn early. A genesis model estimates whether a young depression will become a Cyclonic Storm within 72 h; the RI model estimates the probability of a ≥30 kt jump in the next 24 h and lists the top reasons (SHAP).
  5. Predict. The track model forecasts position and intensity at +6/12/24/48/72 h. Monte-Carlo dropout gives a spread which is scaled so that 67% of real positions fall inside the cone — the same convention as operational cones.
  6. Assess impact. Fifty calibrated forecast members give a landfall probability, most likely country/district and time window; population inside the damaging-wind envelope is counted from WorldPop; fishermen warnings are issued per sea area.
  7. Alert. Coastal districts are staged Watch / Alert / Warning and coloured Yellow / Orange / Red; a CAP 1.2 XML alert (the format NDMA's SACHET system ingests) and short SMS texts in English, Hindi and Gujarati are generated.
Key design principle — replayable, no look-ahead. Every screen uses only data available at the selected time, so any storm since 1990 can be replayed and compared with "what actually happened". This is how the prototype proves it works rather than just showing a dashboard.

How to talk about "success rate" in front of judges. Lead with the three strongest, verifiable numbers: identified 24–42 h before cyclone stage (6/6 storms), 8 of 8 rapid-intensification storms warned (2020–25), and a calibrated uncertainty cone (66% vs 67% target). Then state the limits yourself before a judge asks: track error ≈2× IMD's, detector recall 51% for weak depressions, INSAT domain shift. Never quote a single "accuracy %".

6 · What we built differently

Figure 4 — Compared with the common PS 26070 pattern
  • Starts before the storm exists: basin-wide detection and a genesis probability, not a classifier that needs a pre-cropped storm image.
  • Targets the hardest sub-problem: a dedicated, imbalance-aware rapid-intensification warning with human-readable reasons.
  • Operationally grounded intensity: Dvorak T-number and IMD category alongside the CNN wind, with Grad-CAM.
  • Honest uncertainty: the cone is calibrated and its coverage is measured.
  • Data scarcity addressed: cross-basin transfer learning, measured +14.9%.
  • Closes the loop: landfall, exposure, fishermen warnings, CAP 1.2 XML and multilingual SMS.
  • Proof, not just a demo: replay any storm since 1990 against what actually happened; negative results published.
Careful wording: say "not seen in the public PS 26070 prototypes we reviewed" rather than "no one else does this".

"Is your track better than IMD's?""No — about twice IMD's operational error, because we use track history only, without numerical weather model steering winds. It is decision support; adding GFS steering flow is our next step."
"Did you use INSAT data?""Yes, 132 labelled INSAT-3D images as an external test. Error rises to about 15 kt after calibration because of different image processing — so production training should use native MOSDAC HDF5, which our pipeline is ready for."
"How do you handle class imbalance?""RI is 1 in 15 observations. We use class weighting, calibrate probabilities, choose the threshold on 2017–19 only, and report PR-AUC and false alarms, not accuracy."
"How do you prevent data leakage?""All splits are by storm and year. Tabular models: train to 2016, tune 2017–19, test 2020–25. Image models test on 2016+ storms; the detector on 2017–24 scenes."
"Is it connected to SACHET?""No. It produces CAP 1.2, the standard SACHET ingests, marked status = Exercise. A pilot with NDMA/C-DOT would be the next step."
"What if a satellite channel is missing?""Models are trained with channel dropout. GridSat 2024 has no water vapour: the system switched to IR-only intensity and still detected Cyclone Dana in 80% of fixes."
"Why did the image-sequence model not help?""We tested it fairly against the same network without images: better 24-hour change error, worse RI ranking. So the operational RI warning uses the model that performs best, and we report the negative result."
"Can it run in real time?""Inference takes about one to three seconds per forecast or scene on a laptop CPU. Real-time operation needs a live INSAT or EUMETSAT feed instead of the GridSat archive; the ingest slot is designed for MOSDAC."
"Why use an LLM?""Only to word an optional bulletin from the model's numbers. It is constrained, falls back to a template, and strips any official-agency headers."

Limitations (state them proactively)

  • Decision support only — not an official IMD/NDMA product; CAP status is Exercise.
  • Track skill ≈2× IMD operational error; skill falls quickly beyond 48 h.
  • Domain shift on INSAT JPEGs; no ESCS/SuCS frames in the satellite test year.
  • RI warning also flags some non-RI storms (7 of 19); detector recall is lower for weak depressions.
  • District points and gale radii are illustrative approximations.

Future scope

  • Live INSAT-3D/3DR/3DS ingest from MOSDAC and retraining on native HDF5.
  • GFS/ERA5 steering winds and daily environment fields for track and RI.
  • CAP pilot with NDMA/C-DOT SACHET; more languages (Odia, Tamil, Telugu, Bengali, Marathi).
  • Extension to the whole RSMC New Delhi area; foundation-model track forecasting; physics-informed augmentation for rare extreme storms.
